# Compare one box escape: simulation and robot

The most useful observation is **the first point where the two runs differ**. A robot that hits the doorpost may have started its exit turn too early, turned too far or slipped while crossing. Each leaves different evidence.

1. **Match the setup.** Measure the inside box dimensions, gap width and position. Match these to the sim or record the difference. Pick a starting pose and tape its axle centre and heading on the real floor. For the first comparison use the supplied settings and start without motor calibration in either run; note the sim profile, wall material, floor and lighting. Record the preset and take label (for example, `centre-A`). When the real trial starts, note the test number assigned by the robot against that label.
2. **Save the simulation.** Open [simulator/index.html](simulator/index.html), reproduce the chosen pose, select **Run escape**. The simulator starts recording immediately and skips its virtual calibration bench. Use **Stop trial** to end early, or let the model finish. Keep its numbered final CSV and note the matching physical trial number; the simulation and robot number their tests separately.
3. **Record the robot.** Upload the sketch and open the dashboard as described in the [upload guide](firmware/START_HERE.md). Start an overhead video, place the robot on the physical mark, then press **Start trial**. Do not send `C`. Record whether the entire body clears the gap, touches a post, or stops inside. Let the trial finish or press **Stop trial**. Download its numbered final CSV; link that number to the simulator export and the video in your trial notes.
4. **Find the first mismatch.** Open `/review` on the robot or [review/index.html](review/index.html) offline. Load both CSVs. Compare the same phase and occurrence (for example, the first right turn); the two runs may reach it at different times. Use **Why did the trial end?** to identify which logged signals support a possible explanation, then check it against video, ruler marks or a repeat. A suggestion is not a diagnosis.

On the live dashboard, **Calibration & telemetry** distinguishes what is measured from what is assumed. Estimated x, y and heading come from wheel encoders and the supplied wheel geometry. The three upper IR values are raw counts; the pilot applies its existing thresholds and does not measure distance in centimetres. On a fresh boot the minimum forward power for each motor is unknown unless you explicitly run the optional `C` bench test. An unmeasured setting is normal for this baseline; it does not show that a component is faulty.

| What you saw | What to check in the recording | Independent check |
|---|---|---|
| Rotated too far | Compare estimated heading before the turn and after settling. Look at wheel commands and encoder change as the turn ends. | Measure the same rotation from overhead video or marked headings. Wheel odometry alone cannot tell whether the wheels slipped. |
| Missed the opening or followed a different path | See where the back-right IR changed relative to the transition from wall-following to centring. Compare the estimated paths and phases. | On video, note where the axle was relative to the near post when the robot began turning. A high IR reading alone does not establish an exit. |
| Stopped or did not start | Read the last controller reason and phase; inspect the commands, tick changes and front IR just before the stop. | Check video for contact or a manual stop. A motor command is requested power, not measured wheel speed; a reported reason is what the code saw, not necessarily the underlying cause. |

For example, if both logs estimate a quarter turn but overhead video shows that the physical chassis turned further, record the video angle and the estimated angle at the end of settling. That narrows the next check to encoder geometry, wheel slip or coasting. If both the video and the physical log show the extra turn, inspect when the controller stopped commanding the wheels instead. This is an example of how to use the review, not an observed result.

The number persists across normal robot resets; the **raw rows on the robot** are for the latest attempt in RAM. At roughly five regular samples per second plus state changes, it keeps the newest 512 rows; `trimmed=1` flags lost earlier rows. For a long search, save a partial CSV while it is running to retain early rows; keep the test number and original timestamps so overlapping rows can be identified. A CSV downloaded during motion is a snapshot. After a stop or finish, wait for the browser to archive the final file before another attempt or power-off. Archived files are local to the browser on that device. Download and share them for the group report.

For a report, write: **“At [phase/time], the sim did __; the real robot did __. The log showed __, and the video showed __. Our proposed explanation was __. We changed __ and repeated from the same mark.”** Change one setting per comparison and keep the unsuccessful run alongside the repeat.

**Try the review before the lab:** Open `review/index.html` in a browser and import `review/reference-centre-simulation.csv` as the simulation. This is an export from the packaged Centre/Reference simulation (start: 200 mm, 175 mm, heading 0°). It has no physical measurements. If your real setup uses another start or profile, export a matching simulation instead.

## Repeat physical Test 0002

The robot started with its right side facing the exit. In the [original physical CSV](review/physical-test-0002-original.csv), the front sensor read **600** at 1.100 s while both wheels were commanded to turn. The two left turns settled at encoder-estimated changes of **97.7°** and **98.1°** against a 90° target. Each continued another **4.3°** after its turn command went to zero. Both turns were accepted; neither ended the trial.

At **4.003 s**, the controller stopped with `Opening not confirmed before centring window ended.` The back-right reading was **3880**. Once the projected axle travel from the first possible edge exceeded **30 mm**, the old code abandoned the trial if confirmation was not complete. That separate rule explains the stop. The robot might have passed the doorway, or it might have steered away from the wall: both can weaken the reflection. The CSV alone cannot distinguish them.

Repeat from the **same marked pose**, without calibration. Record the turn headings from above and whether the back-right sensor is facing the doorway or solid cardboard when it reports the edge. If the candidate expires again, look for a return to **SEEK** in the same numbered CSV. That tests the software change. Whether it subsequently follows the wall and physically clears the doorway is a separate observation.

For a matching simulated orientation, choose **Centre**, heading **0°**: the opening is on the robot’s right. Match the actual axle position as closely as you can. New physical exports contain `pilot_revision=opening_retry_v1`; the original file is unchanged.
